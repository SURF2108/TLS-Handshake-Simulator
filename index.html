TLS 1.2 Handshake

How a browser and a server agree on a secret key before any data is sent

How the TLS 1.2 handshake works

1. Hello
Both sides swap random numbers and choose algorithms.
2. Mix secrets
Each side combines a private number with the other’s public one.
3. Shared key
Both reach the same secret. Eve cannot.
Why can’t Eve compute the key?

She sees p, g, A and B, but finding a from A = gᵃ mod p is the discrete logarithm problem. It is easy for tiny numbers and infeasible for 2048-bit ones.

What does the certificate do?

It proves the public values really come from bank.example, so Mallory can’t pretend to be the server.

What does Finished protect against?

Each side sends a MAC of everything it saw. If Mallory changed any earlier message, the hashes differ and the connection is dropped.

What is simplified here?

The RSA-2048 signatures, the three-level certificate chain, the PRF, the key block, Finished and AES-128-CBC with HMAC-SHA256 are all real (computed with your browser’s WebCrypto). Simplified: certificates are plain text instead of X.509, the Diffie-Hellman numbers are small so you can check them by hand, the CBC IV is derived instead of random, and padding is PKCS#7.
